# VOID RUNNER — Beta Readiness PRD

**Status:** draft for review · **Date:** 2026-08-19 · **Baseline commit:** `4e7ab4f` on `main`
**Live build:** https://beyondthevoidrunner.netlify.app/ · **Repo:** NCSTATEPACK16/VoidRunner (public, MIT)

> This document describes what the build **is today**, what "beta ready for public feedback" means
> for it, and the ordered work to get there. It is grounded in the current source tree, not in
> plans — where a feature exists, the file that implements it is named. `PROJECT.md` remains the
> design/tuning source of truth; `PLAN.md` remains the build sequence; this PRD is the *release*

## 1. Product summary

VOID RUNNER is a browser-playable, MIT-licensed, fully open-source first-person tunnel shooter: an
original homage to the 1995 DOS 6DOF lineage ('95 DOS games, *Descent*) rendered with a
Doom-era 320×200 aesthetic. Constant-forward flight through procedurally generated winding
corridors that open into combat arenas, four energy weapons plus a plasma bomb, a shield/energy/heat
economy, and light that dies into black shadow.

**The pitch in one line:** *Doom's weight and Descent's vertigo, in a browser tab, with nothing
imported and nothing to install.*

**Differentiators**
- Zero-friction: click a link, play. No download, no account, no install.
- 100% procedural — every texture, sprite, sound, music cue, and level is generated by code at
  runtime. The repo contains no art files at all.
- Genuinely open source (MIT), including the procedural generation pipeline that is arguably more
  interesting to developers than the game is to players.
- Runs on iPad Safari (Web export built with threads disabled).

**Primary beta audience:** Reddit — Godot/indie developers (interested in the tech), retro-FPS and
DOS-gaming communities (interested in the fidelity), and browser-game players (interested in a
free five-minute hit).

## 3. What "beta ready" means here

Three bars. All three must be met before a Reddit post.

**Bar A — Nobody bounces on infrastructure.** The page loads, explains itself, and takes input
within a few seconds on a cold cache; no freeze, no black screen, no silent failure on a browser
or device a Reddit link will realistically deliver.

**Bar B — Feedback can actually reach me.** There is an obvious, low-friction path from "I have an
opinion" to "the dev has my opinion," and I can tell how many people played versus bounced.

**Bar C — The first ten minutes are defensible.** A player who has never heard of '95 DOS games understands
the controls, survives sector 1, and hits at least one moment they'd describe to a friend.

Everything in §9 is scored against these three bars.

## 4. Known gaps (found by reading the current code, 2026-08-19)

Ordered by beta risk, not by effort.

1. **No touch controls.** A large share of Reddit traffic is mobile. The game is keyboard+mouse
   only; on a phone it is unplayable, and a phone visitor's first impression is a broken game.
   *At minimum* the shell must detect touch-only devices and say "desktop or iPad-with-keyboard
   required" instead of dropping them into an unplayable cockpit.
2. **No photosensitivity warning.** The build has strobe/flicker arena lights, a full white-out on
   plasma bomb detonation, hit-flash, and screen shake. A public beta needs a first-run warning and
   a **flash-reduction toggle** in settings (screen shake is already toggleable; flashing is not).
3. **No feedback path in-game.** Nothing in the build points at a form, an issue tracker, or a
   Discord. Every piece of feedback currently depends on someone bothering to write a Reddit
   comment.
4. **No analytics.** There is no way to distinguish "200 people played" from "200 people opened
   and closed it," which makes all beta feedback uninterpretable.
5. **No difficulty select.** Every player gets the same curve. Retro-authentic difficulty plus a
   cold audience is the classic beta silence generator.
6. **README is stale.** It still reads *"Play it in your browser: (Netlify URL goes here after the
   first deploy)"* — the single most damaging small bug in the repo, since the Reddit post will
   drive people to GitHub.
7. **No open-source on-ramp.** No CONTRIBUTING, no issue templates, no code of conduct, no
   good-first-issues, no public roadmap. An MIT repo without these gets stars, not contributors.
8. **Gamepad support is untested on hardware.** Shipped opt-in and never verified with a physical
   controller.
9. **No in-game build/version stamp.** Bug reports will not be attributable to a build.
10. **No key rebinding, no invert-Y, no FOV slider.** Standard 2026 expectations; their absence is
    a predictable review comment.
11. **Known cosmetic bug:** start-screen sector `< >` nav arrows collide with long sector names
    (visible in `shot_start.png`). Small, visible, on the first screen everyone sees.
12. **Briefing text density.** Three text bands before every level; unmeasured against a cold
    player's patience.
13. **K7 release gates never completed** — the full `PROJECT.md` §8 checklist across all 9 sectors
    and a gauntlet session have not been run end to end.

## 5. Should the files go into a Claude Project?

**Yes — but a curated subset, and the PRD does a different job.** They are complements, not
alternatives:

- **Claude Project = the durable knowledge base.** Put in: this PRD, `PROJECT.md`, `PLAN.md`,
  `CLAUDE.md`, `reference/NOTES.md`, the screenshot-probe images, and a flattened text dump of
  `scripts/` (see below). Then every future Desktop conversation — marketing copy, difficulty
  tuning debates, Reddit replies — starts already knowing the game.
- **This PRD = the shared contract.** It is what you paste into any *new* context (a Reddit mod,
  a collaborator, a fresh Desktop chat, a code-review agent) that will never see the repo.

**Do not** upload: `reference/originals/` (the 1995 shareware files — local only, forever),
`dist/`, `export_templates/`, or `.godot/`.

To generate the code dump for the Project:

```sh
cd void-runner-godot
{ for f in scripts/*.gd resources/**/*.tres project.godot; do
    printf '\n\n===== %s =====\n' "$f"; cat "$f"; done; } > /tmp/voidrunner-source.txt
```

That is roughly 7k lines and fits comfortably in a Project's knowledge base.

## 6. Blender MCP — the right and wrong ways to use it

You have Blender MCP and a 3060 Ti. There is a real opportunity here and a real trap.

**The trap:** importing Blender-authored `.glb` meshes into the game breaks two things at once —
the project's defining "everything is procedural, nothing imported" constraint (which is a genuine
differentiator, not just a purity rule), and the 38 MB web budget that currently makes the
zero-friction pitch work. It would also make the enemies read as smooth modern 3D against a
320×200 dithered background, which is exactly the mismatch that kills retro authenticity.

**The right way — use Blender as a sprite bake farm, the way Doom actually did it.** id Software
sculpted models and photographed/rendered them down into flat sprite sheets. That pipeline maps
perfectly onto this project:

1. Model the enemy or ship in Blender via MCP (`execute_blender_code` — script the whole thing so
   it is reproducible and lives in the repo as a `.py`, not a binary).
2. Light it flat and hard, matching the game's palette lighting.
3. Render an 8- or 16-angle turnaround at 64×64 (the size `sprite_gen.gd` already produces),
   plus a few frames of pain/death.
4. Quantize the output to the game palette from `palette.gd` and write it out as a small
   indexed PNG sheet — a few KB per enemy, not megabytes.

**Two ways to consume the result, and you should pick one deliberately:**

- **Option A (preserves the constraint, recommended):** treat the render as *reference only* — use
  it to improve the procedural generator in `sprite_gen.gd` so the code produces a better silhouette.
  Nothing ships but code. Slower, keeps the story clean.
- **Option B (pragmatic):** ship the baked sheets as generated assets, with the Blender script
  committed alongside so they are reproducible from source. This is still fully open source and
  still nothing from any '95 DOS games, but it changes the README's claim from *"no assets"* to *"all assets
  are generated from committed scripts."* That is a defensible and honestly more impressive claim
  — but it is a **product decision you should make explicitly**, not one to drift into.

**Highest-value targets, in order:** (1) the player ship — a cockpit/chase silhouette is the thing
players look at most and the thing procedural generation is worst at; (2) the three bosses, which
carry the campaign's memorable moments; (3) the hulk and turret, whose silhouettes must read at
distance through fog.

**What the 3060 Ti buys you:** Cycles renders of a 16-angle turnaround in seconds, so this is a
fast iteration loop, not a bottleneck. It is not needed at runtime — the game must keep running on
integrated graphics in a browser.

## 7. Figma — the landing page

Currently the Netlify root *is* the game. That is great for zero-friction play and terrible for a
Reddit post, which needs something to link that survives a skim: what it is, a GIF of it moving,
the pitch, the play button, the repo link, and the feedback link.

**Recommended shape:**
- `/` → a **static landing page** (hand-written HTML/CSS, no framework, no build step — it must not
  complicate `build.sh`), designed in Figma via the Figma MCP and implemented with the
  `figma-design-to-code` skill.
- `/play` → the existing Godot export, untouched.

**Design direction:** the landing page should extend the in-game retro-terminal language that
already exists on the start screen (bracket-boxed panels, `C:VOID_RUNNER> RUN.EXE` prompt, the
navy/black/grey/dark-red palette) rather than inventing a second visual identity. That start-screen
restyle is the design system — build the page from it.

**Must contain:** an autoplaying muted looping GIF/WebM above the fold (this converts more than any
copy), the one-line pitch, PLAY (huge), "what this is" in three sentences, controls, a
requirements/known-issues line (desktop or iPad-with-keyboard; browser game; beta), the GitHub
link, and the feedback link. Keep it one screen plus one scroll.

**Do not** rebuild the in-game start screen in Figma — it lives in a 320×200 SubViewport and its
charm is that it looks like a DOS program. Figma's job stops at the browser page.

## 8. Design direction: "Doom and '95 DOS games, modern"

Framing for the work in §9 and for the Desktop research brief's question 6.

**What to keep from 1995:** the 320×200 chunkiness and palette clamp, light dying into black,
billboarded sprite enemies, the cockpit console framing the view, forgiving wall-bounce damage,
finite ammo on the heavy weapon, and levels that are *places* rather than corridors.

**What must be modern:** onboarding (the first 90 seconds must teach without a manual), readability
(the player must always know where damage came from), difficulty options, accessibility toggles,
checkpointing that respects a browser player's session, and feel — hit-stop, weapon kick, and
audio layering that 1995 hardware could not do and that no modern player will forgive the absence of.

**The synthesis to aim for:** it should *look* like a 1995 screenshot and *feel* like 2026. Every
feature decision should be checkable against that sentence. Where the two conflict, feel wins in
motion and authenticity wins in stills — that is exactly why the screenshot-probe gate exists.

## 10. Success criteria for the beta

The beta succeeds if, within two weeks of the first post:

- ≥ 200 sessions with a measured **sector-1 completion rate above 40%** (below that, onboarding or
  difficulty is broken and that is the finding);
- ≥ 25 pieces of qualitative feedback, of which ≥ 10 are specific enough to become issues;
- zero reports of a hard freeze, black screen, or unplayable browser on desktop;
- at least one outside person opens a GitHub issue or PR.

The beta *fails informatively* if completion rate is low but feedback volume is high — that is a
tuning problem with a clear fix. It fails *uninformatively* if there is no analytics and no
feedback path, which is precisely what blockers B4 and B5 exist to prevent.

## 11. Out of scope for this beta

Steam or itch.io distribution, multiplayer, a tenth secret sector, mobile touch controls beyond
the detection message, level editor or user content, localization, and any Blender-authored
runtime mesh. Each is a real idea; none of them changes whether the first Reddit post goes well.
