Thumbs On Glass

The spike ran at 60 FPS on an iPhone, which answers the only question it was built to answer. Everything that went wrong afterwards is control design and screen real estate — fixable, and specced here.

M4a verdict

Performance passes

Sixty frames a second with a single 17 ms worst frame — one dropped frame across the run. The renderer, the streaming, the sprites and the audio all hold up on a phone with no changes. That was the kill criterion, and it did not trigger.

What it does not prove: a 15 Pro Max is the top of the market. It establishes that nothing in the engine or the level pipeline is structurally too heavy, which is the thing that would have ended mobile outright. A mid-range Android still needs checking before launch, but it is no longer a question of whether the game can run — only how far down the range it goes.

What actually broke

Four defects, each confirmed against the code rather than inferred from the screenshots.

Can't steer and fire at once; ends in a wall Steering is a relative drag — it moves the ship only while the finger is moving. Holding a turn is impossible, so a thumb that pauses to fire stops steering mid-corner. Fundamental
FIRE pad huge and always on screen touch_ui is enabled at level launch and never disabled, so it paints over the game-over screen and menus too. The pad is also 128 px across, sized for a viewport rather than a thumb. Serious

On the console error you saw

undefined is not an object (evaluating 'message.response') is a Safari extension's content script, not the game — the same one that produced the 401s earlier. It appears on every site you visit. The same-origin filter added this session keeps it out of our diagnostics.

The control scheme

Your instinct is right, and the reason is more specific than it looks. Relative drag is position control — the ship turns only while the thumb travels, so a turn ends the moment the thumb stops or lifts. A stick is rate control: hold it over and the ship keeps turning while the other hand does something else. That single change is what makes firing while cornering possible at all.

Left thumb · steer
  • Floating stick. Touch anywhere in the left 45% and the stick's centre is born under your thumb — no hunting for a fixed pad you cannot see while flying.
  • Radius 120 pt, dead zone 8 pt, full deflection = full turn rate. Output feeds the same pitch/yaw path the arrow keys use, so tuning stays shared.
  • D-pad alternative for players who want discrete control, selectable in settings — fixed four-way pad, same corner. Your call which is the default; I'd ship the stick.
  • Lifting recentres instantly. The ship holds heading rather than snapping.
Right thumb · shoot
  • FIRE — 72 pt circle, centred about 90 pt in from the bottom-right corner, inside the natural thumb arc. Down to a third of today's size.
  • WEAPON — 52 pt, above and slightly inboard. Tap cycles; the current weapon's name sits under it.
  • BOMB — 52 pt, inboard of FIRE, with the remaining-charges pips on it. Deliberately the most awkward reach of the three, because it is the rarest and most costly input.
  • Boost is not a button. Held fire already occupies the thumb; boost becomes automatic on a straight run, or a left-zone double-tap. Needs your call.
Why both thumbs can work at once

Every touch carries an index. A touch that begins in the left zone owns steering until it lifts; touches beginning in the right zone are dispatched to whichever button they land on. No input can steal another's finger, which is the actual mechanism behind "fire while turning". The current code half-does this — it tracks indices but allows only one steering finger and treats the whole screen outside the fire pad as steerable.

Gyro, as an option

Worth having, worth defaulting off.

  • What it's good for: fine aim on top of the stick, not replacing it. Tilt maps to a small pitch/yaw offset — roughly ±12° of ship angle at full tilt — so the stick still does the flying and the gyro shaves the last few degrees onto a target. Gyro as primary steering is nauseating on a screen you are also holding.
  • iOS demands a gesture: DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission() must be called from a real tap, and it can be refused. The settings toggle is that tap; a refusal turns the option back off with an honest message rather than silently doing nothing.
  • Calibration: zero is wherever the device is when a level starts, because nobody holds a phone level. A two-finger tap in the left zone re-centres mid-flight.
  • Freed-up thumb: with gyro doing fine aim, the right hand can hold fire continuously — which is the actual argument for the feature.

Screen real estate

The play area is small mostly because Safari is eating the screen, not because the game is scaled wrong. Fix that first and re-judge.

  1. Add to Home Screen. The only reliable full-screen path on iPhone — Safari does not grant the Fullscreen API to arbitrary elements there. With apple-mobile-web-app-capable set, launching from the home screen drops the URL and bookmarks bars entirely and roughly doubles usable height. This is the single biggest win available and costs three meta tags.
  2. Prompt for it. A one-time card on touch devices explaining the Add-to-Home-Screen step, dismissible, shown before the capability card's replacement.
  3. Set stretch/aspect explicitly. Unset today, so Godot letterboxes on all four sides. keep preserves the 1995 framing exactly; expand would fill a 19.5:9 screen but shows more world than desktop players see and would strand the hand-placed cockpit HUD, which is drawn at fixed 320×200 coordinates. Recommend keep, and reclaim the space from Safari instead.
  4. Respect the safe area. viewport-fit=cover plus insets, so nothing lands under the Dynamic Island or the home indicator — which is exactly where a bottom-right fire button wants to sit.
  5. Fix the portrait watcher so the rotate card actually arms, and re-check it whenever touch mode turns on rather than only at load.

Three calls for you

1
Stick or D-pad as the default? A stick gives proportional turn rate and suits flight; a D-pad is more precise for players who dislike floating controls. I would ship the stick and offer the pad. Both get built either way — this is only about which one a first-time player meets.
2
What happens to boost? A fourth button crowds a thumb that is already holding fire. Options: automatic on straight runs, a left-zone double-tap, or dropped on touch entirely. Dropping it changes the game's feel more than it sounds — boost is a big part of the Radix joy.
3
Do we ask people to install it? Add to Home Screen roughly doubles the play area, but it is a step between a Reddit link and playing, and some will not take it. Alternative is to accept a smaller viewport in the browser and offer the install as a hint rather than a gate.
